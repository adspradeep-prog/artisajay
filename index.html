Before there were words,
there were marks.

Original works exploring humanity's oldest visual language — reimagined for the way we live now.

Six series. Limited availability.

Every culture that ever existed independently invented the same handful of marks — the spiral, the grid, the mask, the animal as symbol. Long before anyone could write a sentence, someone was already saying something with a line.

This body of work doesn't belong to one place or one moment. It takes that shared human instinct — the oldest language we have — and speaks it again, in color and form built for the walls we live with today.

Ancient in origin. Contemporary in voice. Made to be lived with, not just looked at.

Entry I — The Animal Marks

Two studies of the same idea.

A creature rendered in two truths at once — a solid, ancient silhouette, and a body made entirely of fractured, brilliant colour. The animal remembers what it was. The colour insists on what it's becoming.

Series of three — available together or individually
Entry II — The Archives

Pages from a book no one wrote down.

The most ambitious works in the collection. Dozens of individual marks, symbols, and forms — boats, suns, figures, spirals — gathered into a single field. Not a painting of one thing. A painting of everything a visual language remembers.

Statement pieces — one available now
Entry III — The Keeper

One creature, carrying the whole archive.

Every inch patterned differently — no two sections alike. A single animal that carries an entire visual language on its back, the way its kind are said to carry memory across generations.

One available now
Entry IV — The Adorned

What people choose to wear closest to the body.

Portraits built around beads, headdresses, woven colour. Not costume — adornment as its own language, worn the same way for as long as anyone has been alive to wear it.

Two available now
Entry V — The Masks

The face, made still.

Weathered, worn, deliberately unplaced in time — these studies feel less painted than found, like objects that existed before the canvas did.

Two available now
The Process

Nothing here is generated, templated, or repeated.

Every piece begins as a hand-drawn field of texture — hours of linework before a single colour is chosen. The pattern is never printed or stencilled. It's built stroke by stroke, the same way it would have been a thousand years ago.
